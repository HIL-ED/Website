An Interactive History Timeline

A House Dividing

The Road to Fort Sumter. Travel from 1793 to 1861 through the real events, real documents, and real people whose choices pulled the North and South further apart, until war finally broke out.

How this works

You'll move through 14 stops in chronological order. At each stop, you'll read what happened, examine an actual primary source (something written or said at the time), and answer a short question about why it mattered.

Three meters

As you move through history, three meters will rise, tracking the widening divide between North and South:

  • Economic Divergence: how differently the Northern and Southern economies were developing.
  • Political Conflict: how strained compromise in Congress and the courts had become.
  • Attitudes on Slavery: how far apart Northern and Southern beliefs about slavery itself had grown.

These meters move based on what actually happened historically, not on your answers. Your answers instead build your Historian Score, a check on how well you understand why each event mattered.

A note on the ending

This isn't a game you can win or lose by making different choices; it's real history, and it ends the way it really ended: with the firing on Fort Sumter. Your job is to understand how the nation got there.

A note before you begin: This game deals honestly with slavery, including some difficult primary sources, because you can't understand the Civil War without understanding slavery. Nothing here is graphic, but some of the words you'll read, especially from people defending slavery, are upsetting on purpose. If you have questions, talk with your teacher or a trusted adult.
