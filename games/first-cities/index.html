🏺 The First Cities

Found a civilization. Choose wisely. It will not always survive.

Before There Were Nations

Long before kingdoms, empires, or borders on a map, small groups of people had to decide: where do we settle, what do we grow or herd, who leads us, and how do we remember what we've learned? Every one of those decisions shaped whether their community thrived, struggled, or vanished from history entirely.

In this game, you make those decisions. You will choose a settlement site, decide how your people domesticate plants and animals, build traditions and technology, and manage resources across twelve turns spanning roughly 2,200 years of ancient history. Along the way, droughts, floods, disease, raiders, and your own people's discontent will test every choice.

This game can end badly. Civilizations in this game can starve, collapse into civil war, be conquered, or wear out the very land they depend on. There is no script forcing success. Your decisions, and the geography you settle in, determine what happens.

Key Ideas You'll Use

Geographic DeterminismWhere you settle shapes almost everything else: your food supply, your defenses, your trade, and the disasters you're most likely to face.
DomesticationTaming plants and animals created reliable food surpluses, but also new risks, like diseases that jump from animals to people.
Language & Record-KeepingKnowledge that only lives in memory can be lost in a single bad generation. Writing changes that, at a cost.
TechnologyTools, irrigation, and construction let a civilization support more people with less land, but they take time and resources to develop.
Tradition & GovernmentHow you're led, what you believe, and how power passes on all shape how well your people hold together under stress.
Rise and FallReal civilizations collapsed from environmental overuse, internal division, and conquest, not just "bad luck." Yours can too.

How to Play

  1. Choose one of eight real-feeling (but fictional) settlement sites. Read each carefully; you cannot move later.
  2. Each turn, allocate your people's labor across the categories available to you. Early on, this is simple: mostly farming and research. As your civilization grows, more categories unlock: building, culture, trade, and military.
  3. Advance the turn to see your harvest, resolve a random event, and sometimes face a bigger decision about your civilization's direction.
  4. Spend accumulated Materials on lasting projects like granaries, irrigation, and walls.
  5. Survive twelve turns to reach a final assessment of your civilization's legacy, or don't. Then reflect on what you'd do differently.

Choose Where to Settle

This is the single most important decision you will make. Click a site on the map to learn about it. Every site trades some advantages for others: no site is simply "the best."
